<h3 class="sec" id="blockchain-digital-markets">Blockchain Governance and Digital Markets</h3>
<p class="sec-q">How do blockchain&rsquo;s distinctive features shape value in digital markets?</p>

<ul>
  <li class="row paper" id="mining-to-meaning">
    <span class="key s1">Accepted</span>
    <div>
      <div class="paper-title">From Mining to Meaning: How Do Blockchain Infrastructure Platforms Respond to Environmental Sustainability Concerns? <span class="tag">Infrastructure</span></div>
      <div class="authors"><strong>Siyuan Jin</strong>, <a href="https://www.allenhuang.org/">Allen H. Huang</a>, Zitong Li, <a href="https://isom.hkust.edu.hk/faculty-and-staff/directory/kytam">Kar Yan Tam</a></div>
      <div class="venue"><em>Journal of Management Information Systems</em> <span class="note">FT50</span></div>
      <div class="actions">
        <a href="https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6975218">Paper</a>
        <a href="/paper/Slides_Green_Token_based_Platform.pdf">Slides</a>
        <details class="abstract"><summary>Abstract</summary>
          <p>We study how environmental concerns shape the value of blockchain infrastructure. After Tesla suspended Bitcoin payments over sustainability concerns, token values fell, especially for energy-intensive proof-of-work platforms. Investors also valued environmental disclosures more positively, and platforms increased those disclosures. Consistent with nonpecuniary preference theory, the findings show how investor awareness can influence decentralized infrastructure through token markets.</p>
          <p class="kw"><strong>Keywords:</strong> blockchain infrastructure, sustainability, nonpecuniary preference</p>
        </details>

  <li class="row paper" id="kpop-dao">
    <span class="key s1">R&amp;R</span>
    <div>
      <div class="paper-title">When K-Pop Meets Blockchain: Consumer Engagement Through Voting in DAOs <span class="tag">Application</span></div>
      <div class="authors"><strong>Siyuan Jin</strong>, <a href="https://isom.hkust.edu.hk/faculty-and-staff/directory/dongwon">Dongwon Lee</a>, <a href="https://www.bschool.cuhk.edu.hk/staff/kim-keongtae/">Keongtae Kim</a>, <a href="https://isom.hkust.edu.hk/faculty-and-staff/directory/kytam">Kar Yan Tam</a></div>
      <div class="venue"><em>Information Systems Research</em>, 2nd round <span class="note">UTD24, FT50</span></div>
      <div class="actions">
        <a href="https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5186789">Paper</a>
        <details class="abstract"><summary>Abstract</summary>
          <p>We study continued participation in a blockchain-based K-pop platform. Smaller voters increase financial and nonfinancial contributions, while large voters whose preferred outcomes lose reduce purchases but maintain nonfinancial engagement. These patterns are consistent with expectation disconfirmation: large voters expect more influence, making losses more disappointing. The findings help explain how voting power becomes less concentrated over time and inform the design of sustainable, consumer-driven platforms.</p>
          <p class="kw"><strong>Keywords:</strong> DAO governance, expectation disconfirmation, consumer engagement</p>
        </details>

  <li class="row paper">
    <span class="key s1">R&amp;R</span>
    <div>
      <div class="paper-title">Supplier Openness and Lower Demand: Evidence from an Invitation-Only Policy Reform in Digital Asset Marketplaces <span class="tag">Application</span></div>
      <div class="authors"><strong>Siyuan Jin</strong>, Ying Hao, <a href="https://www.bschool.cuhk.edu.hk/staff/li-hongfei/">Hongfei Li</a>, <a href="https://www.bschool.cuhk.edu.hk/staff/kim-keongtae/">Keongtae Kim</a>, <a href="https://isom.hkust.edu.hk/faculty-and-staff/directory/kytam">Kar Yan Tam</a></div>
      <div class="venue"><em>Information Systems Research</em>, 3rd round <span class="note">UTD24, FT50</span></div>
      <div class="actions">
        <a href="https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5535298">Paper</a>
        <details class="abstract"><summary>Abstract</summary>
          <p>Can more primary-market supply reduce demand? We study Foundation&rsquo;s removal of invitation-only onboarding, using SuperRare as a comparison. After entry opened, bids and listings declined, with larger bidding declines among active traders. The evidence is consistent with a resale channel: new primary listings compete with secondary listings, reducing expected resale opportunities. The study shows how primary-market entry policy can reshape secondary-market competition and demand.</p>
          <p class="kw"><strong>Keywords:</strong> digital assets, primary-market policy, secondary-market competition</p>
        </details>
